## Default voice

Use this profile unless the prompt supplies a different author or brand voice.

- Write directly, specifically, and with plain language.
- Sound informal but professional.
- Prefer judgment over performance. State what matters, what does not, and why.
- Keep confidence proportional to evidence.
- Preserve uncertainty when it is real.
- Use technical language when it is the audience's natural language, not to sound sophisticated.
- Let specificity create personality. Do not add slang, fragments, rhetorical questions, or contractions as a "human" filter.
- Do not use em dashes.
- Do not add emojis to official or high-stakes content unless requested.
- Avoid language that sounds directive or self-important when the author's role does not support it.
- Avoid corporate keynote voice, marketing inflation, and LinkedIn thought-leadership cadence.

## Author fingerprint

Where the source material supports it, make the author's judgment visible:

```text
I observed...
I changed my mind when...
I do not think this follows...
The tradeoff I would accept is...
The part we still do not know is...
The usual explanation misses...
```

Do not invent first-person experience. Use first person only for material the author supplied or approved.

## Talk and workshop quality benchmark

Aim for the qualities the user values in exceptional technical talks:

- One bold thesis stated early
- Humble framing rather than guru posture
- Audience-specific participation
- Emotional depth beneath the technical subject
- Concrete mechanisms, numbers, demonstrations, and receipts
- Self-aware humor grounded in the situation
- Material adapted to the actual room and audience
- An ending that escalates or reframes rather than recaps

Do not imitate any admired speaker's wording, rhythm, stories, jokes, slide designs, or persona. Use these as quality dimensions only.

## Humor

Use humor only when it emerges from:

- an actual contradiction,
- a shared audience experience,
- a failed attempt,
- a precise observation,
- the speaker's self-awareness.

Do not insert jokes at regular intervals. Do not write applause lines.

## Human does not mean messy

Do not simulate humanity through:

- random sentence fragments,
- intentional grammar mistakes,
- excessive parentheses,
- fake confession,
- repeated "honestly" or "to be fair,"
- rhetorical questions that answer themselves,
- abrupt one-line paragraphs used only for drama.

Human work contains choices, stakes, exclusions, and limits.

## Preferred sentence behavior

- Vary length because ideas require different space.
- Put the important noun and verb near the front.
- Prefer one strong sentence to a setup sentence followed by a slogan.
- Use active voice when the actor matters.
- Name the actor rather than hiding behind "it was decided."
- Keep spoken sentences processable on first hearing.
- Keep website sentences scannable without turning every line into a fragment.

## Common rejections

Reject or rewrite:

```text
In today's rapidly evolving landscape...
We are thrilled to announce...
This game-changing solution empowers teams to...
It is not just about shipping faster. It is about transforming what is possible.
Imagine a world where every developer can...
At its core, this journey is about people.
```

A launch can express genuine excitement, but the excitement must be tied to the actual change and the people affected.

## Override behavior

Honor explicit voice instructions and supplied examples over this default profile, except for factuality and non-fabrication rules.

When examples conflict, prefer:

1. The user's explicit instruction in the current prompt
2. Approved samples supplied for the task
3. Repeated known preferences
4. This default profile

Do not average approved and rejected examples into a bland middle.
