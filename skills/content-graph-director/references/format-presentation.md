## Treat the deck as two connected systems

1. Visual sequence: what the audience sees.
2. Spoken narrative: what the presenter says, reveals, and withholds.

Do not put the full narration on the slides.

## Classify the deck

- Thought leadership or keynote
- Educational or technical
- Product or launch narrative
- Workshop support deck
- Executive or business review
- Sales, partner, or proposal deck

Use the selected content job to determine the narrative, not the template category alone.

## Narrative spine

Record:

```text
audience starting belief or question
opening tension
major turns
proof moments
participation or demonstration moments
final decision or reframe
```

Read slide titles alone. They should reveal movement, not a table of contents.

## Slide jobs

Assign one primary job to every slide:

- Establish
- Orient
- Claim
- Prove
- Demonstrate
- Contrast
- Reveal
- Involve
- Pause
- Reframe
- Decide
- Resolve

Delete slides that only decorate, repeat, or announce a section.

## Slide contract

For each slide, define:

```text
job
audience takeaway
visual form
on-slide text
speaker contribution
transition to next slide
source if needed
```

Use a visual only when it helps the audience understand evidence, sequence, scale, contrast, architecture, or emotion. Do not use decorative stock imagery to simulate polish.

## On-slide rules

- Keep one primary idea per slide.
- Avoid title plus three bullets as the automatic layout.
- Prefer a number, quote, diagram, screenshot, example, sequence, or contrast when it carries the claim better than bullets.
- Keep text readable in the actual room.
- Avoid paragraphs unless the slide is intentionally showing a document or quotation.
- Do not duplicate the speaker notes.
- Use brand styling consistently, but do not let branding replace meaning.

## Speaker notes

Use notes to add what the slide cannot:

- Explanation
- Story
- Mechanism
- Caveat
- Source detail
- Timing
- Audience prompt
- Transition

Write notes for speech, not as an essay. Mark stage directions separately.

## Evidence placement

- Put the core number or proof on the slide when it is the point.
- Put detailed citations in notes or a sources section when visual clutter would harm comprehension.
- Never create a chart from unsupported or invented data.
- Preserve axes, units, baselines, and relevant context.

## Common deck failures

Reject:

- Agenda slides that add no orientation
- Repeated section-divider slides
- A slide for every paragraph
- Generic icon grids
- Decorative diagrams with no explanatory value
- A recap that repeats the entire talk
- Speaker notes that merely read the slide
- Tiny citations or text that cannot be read in the room

## Native artifact behavior

When the user requests a PPTX or actual presentation file:

1. Complete the narrative and slide contracts.
2. Use the available presentation capability.
3. Include speaker notes when requested or when the deck supports a talk.
4. Render and inspect the deck for overflow, legibility, alignment, repetition, and visual pacing.
5. Fix layout problems before delivery.

## Text-only deliverable

When no native file is requested, use this adaptable structure:

```text
Slide [number]: [working title]
Job:
Visual:
On-slide text:
Speaker notes:
Transition:
Source:
```

Omit fields that genuinely add no value. Do not reduce the output to titles and bullet points when the user asked for a complete deck narrative.
