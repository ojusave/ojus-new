Select the job before selecting a structure. Medium alone does not determine quality.

## Argue or change a belief

Use when the audience should leave believing something different.

Required state:

```text
current belief
proposed belief
tension or contradiction
mechanism
evidence
reasonable objection
limitation
decision or implication
```

Pass tests:

- The claim is specific enough to disagree with.
- The audience does not already accept it in the same form.
- Evidence supports the claim at the stated level of certainty.
- The conclusion changes a decision, interpretation, or priority.

Do not manufacture contrarianism. A precise useful claim is better than a dramatic false one.

## Teach or enable

Use when the audience must perform a task or understand a system well enough to act.

Required state:

```text
learner starting point
target capability
prerequisites
sequence
verification
common failure modes
recovery
next useful task
```

Pass tests:

- The learner can tell whether they succeeded.
- The sequence reflects actual dependencies.
- Examples are executable or concrete.
- Failure recovery is included where mistakes are likely.

Do not force a bold thesis. Clarity, correctness, and task completion matter more.

## Persuade or convert

Use when the audience must choose, sign up, buy, attend, adopt, approve, or support.

Required state:

```text
audience need or risk
promise
mechanism
proof
objections
tradeoffs
action
cost of action and inaction
```

Pass tests:

- The promise is precise and credible.
- Proof sits near the claim it supports.
- The CTA matches the audience's readiness.
- Material tradeoffs are not hidden.

Do not replace proof with adjectives.

## Announce or update

Use for launches, changes, incidents, milestones, event information, and status updates.

Required state:

```text
what changed
who is affected
when it changed
why it matters
exact behavior or availability
required action
known limitations
where to get updates
```

Pass tests:

- A reader can identify the change and next step quickly.
- Dates and availability are exact.
- The announcement does not claim impact that has not been measured.
- Important limitations are visible.

Do not inflate routine changes into revolutions.

## Facilitate participation

Use when participants must think, decide, discuss, practice, create, or diagnose during the experience.

Required state:

```text
participant outcome
physical or verbal action
materials and room constraints
prompt
artifact or decision produced
timebox
debrief
transfer to real work
failure mode and recovery
```

Pass tests:

- Participants do something observable.
- The activity can work in the stated room and time.
- Instructions are understandable before the activity starts.
- The debrief extracts a reusable principle.

A talk with discussion questions is not automatically a workshop.

## Explain or compare

Use for explainers, product comparisons, decision guides, definitions, and analysis.

Required state:

```text
question or decision
terms
criteria
options or components
mechanisms
tradeoffs
boundaries
recommendation conditions
```

Pass tests:

- Comparison criteria are consistent.
- Differences are meaningful, not cosmetic.
- Recommendations include the conditions under which they change.
- Unknowns are identified.

Do not create false balance when evidence clearly favors one choice.

## Narrate or inspire

Use when a story or emotional movement helps the audience reinterpret an experience, imagine a possibility, or commit to action.

Required state:

```text
human situation
change or conflict
specific moments
stakes
meaning earned from events
connection to audience
ending movement
```

Pass tests:

- Emotion comes from concrete events, not elevated adjectives.
- The story changes the audience's understanding.
- Vulnerability is real and relevant, not decorative.
- The ending advances the meaning instead of repeating it.

Do not manufacture intimacy or inspiration.

## Common combinations

### Thought leadership talk

Primary: argue or change belief.
Secondary: narrate or inspire.

### Technical tutorial blog

Primary: teach or enable.
Secondary: explain or compare.

### Product launch page

Primary: persuade or convert.
Secondary: announce or update.

### Conference workshop

Primary: facilitate participation.
Secondary: teach or enable.

### Product keynote

Primary: persuade or convert.
Secondary: narrate or inspire, then announce or update.

### Incident post

Primary: announce or update.
Secondary: explain.

Do not let the secondary job displace the primary outcome.
