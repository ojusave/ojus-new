## Classify the article

Choose the closest subtype:

- Opinion or argument
- Analysis or explainer
- Tutorial or how-to
- Launch or announcement
- Case study
- Comparison or decision guide
- SEO-led educational article

Do not impose the same structure on every subtype.

## Opinion or argument graph

```text
reader belief
-> precise claim
-> tension or observation
-> mechanism
-> evidence
-> objection
-> limitation
-> implication
```

Require a claim that can be disagreed with. Do not mistake a topic for a thesis.

## Analysis or explainer graph

```text
question
-> relevant terms
-> system or mechanism
-> evidence or examples
-> boundaries
-> implications
```

Optimize for understanding. Do not force a dramatic stance.

## Tutorial graph

```text
target result
-> prerequisites
-> first success
-> complete steps
-> verification
-> likely errors
-> recovery
-> next task
```

Use exact commands, inputs, outputs, and expected states when available. Do not bury the first useful result behind background exposition.

## Launch or announcement graph

```text
what changed
-> who it affects
-> exact capability
-> why it was built
-> how to use it
-> availability and limits
-> next action
```

Do not claim market impact, customer enthusiasm, or performance gains without evidence.

## Case study graph

```text
starting situation
-> constraint
-> decision
-> implementation
-> measured result
-> confounders or limitations
-> transferable lesson
```

Never invent a customer, quote, metric, or causal explanation.

## Structure rules

- Start with the claim, event, task, or concrete problem.
- Give every section one job.
- Keep background only when the reader needs it for the next step.
- Place evidence near the claim it supports.
- Use examples that reveal a mechanism or decision.
- Make transitions follow the thought rather than announce the outline.
- Use headings for navigation, not to make an empty taxonomy look complete.
- End by resolving the question, changing the decision, or giving the next useful action.

## Paragraph audit

For each paragraph, identify its function:

```text
claim
evidence
mechanism
example
instruction
objection
limitation
transition with new information
consequence
action
```

Delete or combine paragraphs with no function.

## Title behavior

Create titles after the article's actual claim is known.

Prefer:

- A precise claim
- A concrete question
- A decision the reader faces
- A named mechanism or outcome

Avoid vague drama, unsupported superlatives, and clickbait that the article does not earn.

## SEO behavior

Apply SEO after the useful article exists.

- Preserve the reader's language in titles and headings where natural.
- Answer the search intent early.
- Add definitions only when the reader needs them.
- Do not repeat keywords mechanically.
- Do not expand the article with generic sections merely to increase length.
- Distinguish search coverage from argument structure.

## Default deliverable

When the user asks for a finished blog or article, return:

1. Title
2. Optional subtitle only when useful
3. Complete article
4. Brief source or placeholder note only when needed

Do not return only an outline unless requested.
